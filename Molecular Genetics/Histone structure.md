## Nucleosome

nucleosomoe core is an octamer of sets of 2 of each of histones H2A, H2B, H3, H4
N-ter. of the proteins point out of the structure


H1 - linker histone

dna wraps around histones twice

wrapping around a histone gives 2\*pi = ~6 times contensation

nucleosome - 8 histones +2 rounds of dna?

arrangement of histones is passed down to daughter cells


octamer assembled out of 1 H3-H4 tetramer and 2 H2A-H2B dimers


histones are small, positively-charged, highly conserved
attracted to negatively charged dna


posttranslational modifications of histones

4 core histone tails can be modified
more acetyl groups - more condensed
methylation is position-dependent

H3K9 methylation - gene silencing, compaction
histone-lysine hyper-acetylation correlates with open chromatin structure - neutralising the charge of the lysine

acetylation → open; transcription
deacetylation → closed; repression



histone code
chromo domains on proteins recognise methylated lysines on histones


## Non-histone proteins

roles:
structural role - chromosome scaffold
chromosome replication - dna polymerases
chromosome segregation - kinetochore proteins
active in transcription



HP-1 (heterochromatin protein 1) can recognise the methylated lysine on histones
if 2 HP-1 proteins attach to methyl groups on adjacent histones, they can dimerise among themselves & bring the whole structure together, condensing it.
HP-1 can also recruit HKMT to methylate another histone tail and spread the whole structure


inactivated X chromosome in females contains a lot of H3K9 methylation

#### Finding regions without nucleosomes

euchromatin - region that gets decondensed in interphase; less histones.
heterochromatin - region that doesn't decondense in interphase; more histones; e.g. deactivated X chromosome in females.
	constitutive - condensed in all cells
	facultative - condensed only in some cells
