## Chromosomes

**Haploid cells** - contain unpaired chromosomes; gametes.
**Diploid cells** - contain paired chromosomes; somatic cells.

homologous chromosomes - paired-up chromosomes; contain the same genes, even if different alleles.
sister chromatids - identical; after replication
centromere - where the sister chromatids are attached

chromosomes:
metacentric - centrosome in the middle
acrocentric - centrosome closer to one side
telocentric - centrosome at the end of the chromosome.

> Humans have 46 chromosomes.

##  Mitosis

Generates 2 daughter cells w/ same \# and type of chromosomes as in the parent cell.
Produces diploid cells.


in G1, G2 cell growth
G for "gap"

G1 - cell growth
S phase - duplication of chromosomes into sister chromatids
G2 phase - cell growth, synthesis of proteins required for mitosis.

phases:
- prophase - chromosomes condense & become visible
- prometaphase - nuclear envelope breaks down, can use the whole cell for replication, spindle forms and sister chromatids attach to microtubules from opposite centrosomes
- metaphase - chromosomes align at the cell equator (metaphase plate); cohesins hold sister chromatids
- anaphase - sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles; separase breaks the cohesins
- telophase - spindle disappears, chromosomes decondense and are enclosed in two nuclei
- cytokinesis - cytoplasm divides & produces 2 daughter cells

no cytokinesis is cells w/ multiple nuclei
syncytium





## Meiosis

Generates gametes containing half of the usual \# of chromosomes.
Produces haploid cells.

chromosomes duplicate once (like in mitosis), nuclei divide twice

Meiosis I ("reductional division") - halves the \# of chromosomes

homologous chromosomes can cross over (exchange DNA between themselves)


Meoisis II ("equational division") - produces 4 haploid cells


## Relevance

Meiosis contributes to genetic diversity:
- intependent assortment
- cross-over between homologs
