
**Gene** - discrete unit of information that defines a trait.
**Allele** - variant of a gene on a chromosome.
**Homozygous** - having the same alleles of the same gene.
**Heterozygous** - having different alleles of the same gene.

In heterozygous:
	**Dominant** - is expressed; determines the phenotype.
	**Recessive** - is not expressed.

**Genotype** - the collection of all of the genes of an organism.
**Phenotype** - the collection of all of the visible traits of an organism.

**Hybridization** - mating or cross between 2 individuals w/ different characteristics.
**Hybrid** - offspring of hybridisation.

**P** (parent) - parent generation.
**F1** (1st filial) - first-generation offspring.

**True-breeder/strain/line** - produces the same genotype in offspring as parents over many generations; homozygous.
**Monohybrid cross** - hybrid between organisms that differ in only one trait (or care about only one trait at the time).

## Mendel's laws

**1. Mendel's Law of Segregation**
Individual contains 2 alleles of each trait and they segregate randomly in its gametes.

**2. Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment**
During gamete formation the segregation of any pair of hereditary determinants (alleles) is independent of segregation of the other.

> Only true, if the genes are on different chromosomes.
> ==link to source in another file==

**3. Mendel's Law of Domination**
If an individual has different alleles (heterozygous individual), then one allele defines the phenotype (is dominant) and the other is not expressed (is recessive.

## Punnett square

Diagram showing the combinations of alleles the offspring may possess.

#### Example 1 - monohybrid cross

1. Genotype of both parents (P):
   ♂:Tt
   ♀: Tt
2. Possible gametes from both parents:
   ♂: T or t
   ♀: T or t
3. Create the Punnett square:

| ♀/♂   | T   | t   |
| ----- | --- | --- |
| **T** | TT  | Tt  |
| **t** | Tt  | tt  |
4. Determine the relative proportion of genotypes & phenotypes in F1:
   
| Genotype    | TT   | Tt   | tt    |
| ----------- | ---- | ---- | ----- |
| **Proportion**  | 1    | 2    | 1     |
| **Probability** | 25%  | 50%  | 25%   |
| **Phenotype**   | Tall | Tall | Short |

#### Example 2 - dihybrid cross

Trait 1 - seed texture, round (R) vs wrinkled (r).
Trait 2 - seed colour, yellow (Y) vs green (y).

P:   RRYY vs rryy
F1:         RrYy       - let self-fertilise; F1 x F1 cross
F2:         ????

Possible gametes:
	♂: RY Ry rY ry
	♀: RY Ry rY ry

| ♀/♂    | RY   | Ry   | rY   | ry   |
| ------ | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| **RY** | RRYY | RRYy | RrYY | RrYy |
| **Ry** | RRYy | RRyy | RrYy | Rryy |
| **rY** | RrYY | RrYy | rrYY | rrYy |
| **ry** | RrYy | Rryy | rrYy | rryy |

| Genotype | Ratio |        | Phenotype ratio       |
| -------- | ----- | ------ | --------------------- |
| RRYY     | 1     | R\_Y_  | 9/16 round; yellow    |
| RRYy     | 2     | R\_Y\_ |                       |
| RrYY     | 2     | R\_Y\_ |                       |
| RrYy     | 4     | R\_Y\_ |                       |
| RRyy     | 1     | R\_yy  | 3/16 round; green     |
| Rryy     | 2     | R\_yy  |                       |
| rrYY     | 1     | rrY\_  | 3/16 wrinkled; yellow |
| rrYy     | 2     | rrY\_  |                       |
| rryy     | 1     | rryy   | 1/16 wrinkled; green  |

> In total there should be 3 \* 3 = 9 genotypes.
> In [[#Example 1 - monohybrid cross]] you can see that for 2 heterozygous individuals 3 distinct genotypes are produced.

If the 2 genes **assort independently**, we expect 9:3:3:1 ratio in F2.

> Notice how the genotypes that come up only once are on the top-left-to-bottom-right diagonal and all the others are on bottom-left-to-top-right diagonals.

> To get the \# of ways a genotype may appear, assume the different genes are mixed independently ([[#Example 1 - monohybrid cross]]).
> Then multiply the \# of ways one pair of alleles can appear by the \# of ways the other pair of alleles can appear.

## Extensions to Mendel

**Incomplete dominance** - in heterozygous individuals the phenotype is in between of a purely dominant homozygous & purely recessive homozygous.
Example: cross between red & ivory snapdragons is pink.
Molecular reason: rate-limiting enzyme is encoded by the dominant allele.

**Codominance** - can see both alleles; both are dominant.
Example: in blood groups can see both antigens on red blood cells at the same time.

![[types-of-dominance.png]]

There are often more than 2 alleles in the population.
The dominance relationship is unique to a pair of alleles.

$\text{C} < \text{c}^{\text{ch}} < \text{c}^{\text{c}} < \text{c}$

> Dominance is always relative!

> Dominance is usually a transitive relationship.

**Pleiotropy** - 1 gene influencing several traits.
Gene product may be:
- has several functions
- expressed in different cell types
- expressed at different growth stages

Some allele combinations are lethal.
Example: sickle cell anemia.

**Penetrance** - percentage of individuals w/ a particular genotype that show the expected phenotype.
Dominant alleles are not always expressed in heterozygotes.
Expressed in 60% of heterozygotes: penetrance = 60%.
In 1 individual we say the gene is either **penetrant** or **not** - not a percentage.

**Expressivity** - the degree to which a trait is expressed.

**Gene redundancy** - 1 gene compensates for the loss of another.
When knock out a gene and no obvious phenotype is observed.
To check for gene redundancy, need to get the aabb individual.
Use [[#Breeding dihybrid genotypes]], get 15:1 ratio with 1 being the aabb genotype.

**Epistasis** - expression of one gene is modified/masked/inhibited by 1 or more other genes.

Reciprocal recessive epistasis - one recessive gene suppresses another gene; ratio: 9:7
Molecular explanation: need a protein (dominant allele) from both genes at the same time.
Dominant genotype: A\_B\_
Recessive genotype: aaB\_, A\_bb, aabb

![[reciprocal-recessive-epistasis.png|492]]

Dominant epistasis - one dominant gene suppresses another gene; ratio: 13:3
Molecular explanation: protein B blocks the phenotype by protein A.
Dominant genotype: A\_bb
Recessive genotype: aaB\_, A\_B\_, aabb

![[dominant-epistasis.png|453]]

Recessive epistasis - one recessive gene suppressed another gene; ratio: 9:3:4

**Sex-linked inheritance** - when the alleles are on [[Sex Chromosomes|sex chromosomes]].

**Hemizygous** - only having 1 allele; for a male ♂ that has the allele on the X chromosome (Y is just for pairing and is ignored).
Also applicable if the allele on another chromosome is deleted through a mutation.
**Autosomal inheritance** - the allele is on any chromosome that's not a sex chromosome.

#### Continuous variation

Mendel's genes are discontinuous - **either or**.
Continuous variation in the phenotype usually when many genes interact together.
**Quantitative traits** - means that they can be measured (e.g. body length, skin colour, strength, etc.).
The more genes & alleles there are in the population, the more the traits approach the normal distribution.
