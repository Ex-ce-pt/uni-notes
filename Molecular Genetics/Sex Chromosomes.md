XX - male
XY - female

Sex inheritance would be a XX x XY cross.
Results in approximately 1:1 ratio of sexes in the offspring.

Primary determinant of maleness is a single gene SRY (sex determining region of Y).
Sex reversal can happen if SRY moves between the sex chromosomes: XX* male, XY* female. ==moving in meiosis==
SRY **always** present in XX* males.
SRY is **always** nonfunctional in XY* females.

**PAR** (pseudoautosomal region) - contains the genes other than sex genes.
Helps to pair up in meiosis.
Located towards the ends of the Y chromosome.

X chromosome contains most important genes; no X chromosome → death.
Y chromosome has few genes.

## Spermatogenesis

Happens throughout the male life.
**Spermatogonia** - diploid germ cells found only in testes.
**Spermatogonia** → mitosis → **primary spermatocytes** → symmetrical meiosis I → **secondary spermatocytes** → symmetrical meiosis II → spermatids → maturation → **sperm**.

Entire process takes 48-60 days.

## Dosage problem

Females have 2 times more X chromosomes than males → more transcription.
Multiple ways to solve.
In humans - deactivation of redundant X chromosomes.
Deactivated chromosomes are condensed into Barr bodies.
Each cell of the embryo decides by itself which chromosome is deactivated - around 9th day after egg-sperm fusion.
Exception: in trophectoderm, maternal - **always** active, paternal - deactivated.
Since all but 1 X chromosome are deactivated, XXX and XXXX are essentially normal females.

Other species have other ways of solving the problem:
- Deactivation of redundant X chromosomes (like in humans).
- Upregulating the transcription of singular male X chromosome by 2 times.
- Downregulating transcription of both female X chromosomes by a half.

## Nomenclature

Usually the genes are named after than happens when the gene is disabled.
Dominant mutant allele denoted with uppercase.
Recessive mutant alleles denoted in lowercase.
Wild-type allele denoted with a superscript +.
