
**Antimicrobials** - chemical compounds that kill/prevent the growth of microbes.
	**Antibiotics, Antifungals, Antiprotozoals, Antivirals** - what it says it is.

Origin:
- **Natural** - produced by microbes/plants.
- **Semisynthetic** - derivatives of naturals.
- **Synthetic** - fully manmade.

Specificity:
- **Broad spectrum** - kill various species of microbes; for prophylaxis or polymicrobial infections.
- **Narrow spectrum** - kill a specific group of microbes; if available & species of pathogen is known.

Mode of action:
- **Microbiostatic/bacteriostatic** - prevent growth; reversible.
- **Microbiocidal/bacteriocidal** - kill.
- **Microbiolytic/bacteriolytic** - lyse.

> Same antibiotic can have a different mode of action depending on the concentration/environment.

Efficacy:
- **Concentration-dependent** - more effective when administered in large doses, even if for a little time.
- **Time-dependent** - the more effective the longer it's taken.

Interactions:
- **Additive** - 2 drugs work together, essentially ignoring each other.
- **Antagonistic** - one of the drugs looses efficiency.
- **Synergistic** - 2 drugs amply each other's effects.

Targets:
- Cell wall
- Cell membrane
- DNA synthesis
- Ribosomes
- Metabolic pathways
- Proteins (e.g. reverse transcriptase against viruses)
- ATP synthesis

Routes of administration:
- **Oral** - swallowing, get absorbed in the GI tract.
- **Topical** - applied to skin/mucosa.
- **Parenteral** - straight into veins or muscle tissue; when oral not possible or doesn't get absorbed in the GI tract.

## Tests

#### Kirby-Bauer disk diffusion test

Antibiotic placed on a filter paper in an LA plate.
If bacteria grow everywhere on the plate except for around the filter paper, antibiotic works.
No information about efficiency or bacteriostatic/bacteriocidal.

#### Dilution test

Add antibiotic to suspended bacteria in different concentrations.
**MIC (minimal inhibitory concentration)** - lowest drug concentration that inhibits **visible** growth.
**MBC (minimal bactericidal concentration)** - lowest drug concentration to kill >99.9% of bacteria.

#### Etest

A combination; in hospitals.
Plastic strip has a gradient of antibiotic concentration.
Strip placed in a culture (lawn of bacteria).
Can see effects of different concentrations.
Can see MIC, but not MBC.

#### Genotypic analysis

Detecting antibiotic resistance.
Cannot get MIC or MBC.

## Dangers

**Superinfection** - second infection that comes after the patient's microbiota is disrupted in the process of curing the previous one w/ antibiotics.
**Antimictobial resistance**.
