
All use of antimicrobials promotes resistance.

Antibiotic resistance has no advantage or is disadvantageous at lower concentrations - stopping using antibiotic entirely would at some point remove all the resistance.

**Co-selection** - one plasmid accumulates multiple resistance genes to multiple antimicrobials while shared via [[Horizontal gene transfer#Conjugation|conjugation]].

In lower-income countries antibiotics are more widely available without prescription - overuse.

> DO NOT USE ANTIBIOTICS AGAINST VIRUSES - misuse.

If broad spectrum antibiotics are used and resistance for them is developed, we are doomed.
Always use the narrowest spectrum antibiotic appropriate.

In hospital settings AMR is especially important:
- Immunocompromised patients.
- Variety of pathogens.
- Prophylactic antibiotics.

Livestock & agricultural industries also use prophylactic antibiotics a lot - resistant pathogens may transfer to humans.

**Antibiotic pollution** - from agriculture or waste water.
Water treatment facilities use ozone to destroy the medicine.
Antibiotic production facilities have high concentrations of antibiotics around them.

**MSC (minimal selective concentration)** - lowest concentration at which resistant mutant grow faster than susceptible strains.
**Mutant selective window** - range of concentrations where the resistant mutants outcompete susceptible strains.

> MSC is (potentially several hundred times) lower than the MIC of susceptible strains.
> Don't have to die to be outcompeted (select for the mutants).

WHO has a priority list for pathogens that new antibiotics are urgently needed for.

## Antibiotics alternatives

#### Anti-virulence therapy

Targets pathogen's virulence factors.
Don't kill - disarm.

Pros:
- Less resistance.
- Narrow spectrum; less harmful to microbiota.

Cons:
- In clinical trials.

#### Phage therapy

Use bacteriophages to kill bacteria they naturally prey upon.
Can use natural or genetically engineered phages.

Pros:
- Bacteriocidal.
- Narrow spectrum.
- Co-evolution - less resistance.
- Synergism w/ antibiotics.
- Available for some pathogens.

Cons:
- Only a few countries offer.
- Mostly in clinical trials.
- Immune system overreaction possible.

#### Lysins

Phage proteins that degrade cell wall.
**Ectolysins** - used to get phage genome into the bacterium.
**Endolysins** - used to degrade the cell from inside after phage replication.

Pros:
- See [[#Phage therapy]]

Cons:
- Mostly in clinical trials.
- Mostly against Gram$^+$ bacteria - harder w/ Gram$^-$.

#### CRISPR

CRISPS/Cas systems - adaptive immunity in bacteria; degrades foreign DNA.

Pros:
- Narrow spectrum.
- Possible to engineer to target specific sequences.

Cons:
- Early development.

#### Predators

Introduce bacteria that prey on the pathogen.

#### Bacteriocins

Diffusable antimicrobial protein toxins.

#### Host-directed therapy (HDT)

Alert host's immune system instead of killing bacteria.

Pros:
- Can't develop resistance, because the pathogen is not targeted.

Cons:
- Side effects.
