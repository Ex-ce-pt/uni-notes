
## Genomics

**Genome** - all the genes of an organism.
	**Core genome** - essential genes present in all organisms of a species.
	**Accessory genome** - present only in some organisms of a species.
	**Pangenome** - union of all gene families of a species.

**1st gen. sequencing** - short segments, very accurate; see [[Genetic engineering#Sanger sequencing (chain termination method)|Sanger sequencing]].
**2nd gen. sequencing** - massively parallel, lots of chopped pieces of the genome, fairly accurate; Illumina.
**3rd gen. sequencing** - long read sequencing, decently accurate; Oxford nanopore.

Usually the genome is cut into lots of small pieces that can be easily sequenced and then via alignment of the base pairs the entire genome is reconstructed.
Of course, sometimes some base pairs get lost in sequencing.

## Transcriptomics

**Transcriptome** - all the RNA of an organism.

Usually use reverse transcriptase to get the complementary DNA strand & sequence it, see [[#Genomics]].
Doesn't allow to reconstruct the whole genome - no promoters + introns get lost in eukaryotes.
Tells gene expression.
Also influenced by epigenetics.

## Proteomics

**Proteome** - all the proteins of an organism.
Differs cell-to-cell, time-to-time.
\+ post-translational modifications exist.

No easy way to sequence proteins.
Proteins separated using SDS-PAGE, western blotting or ELISA (marker antigens bind to proteins).
Can also cut proteins into short peptides and run mass spectroscopy.
X-ray crystallography & Cryo-EM are used to create protein crystals & get the structure.

## Metabolomics

**Metabolome** - all the metabolites of an organism.

Shows the current state of the cell.
Usual chemical techniques: MS, NMR, etc.
