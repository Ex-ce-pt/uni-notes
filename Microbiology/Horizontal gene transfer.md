
**Vertical gene transfer** - through generations; parent → daughters.
**Horizontal gene transfer** - within a generation; peer → peer.

Important for bacteria to acquire adaptations.
Drives evolution.
New pathogens emerge.

## Transformation

Uptake of naked DNA from the environment.
Both Gram-$(+)$ or Gram-$(-)$

DNA can be used for:
- Generating genetic diversity
- DNA repair
- Nutrients (by degradation into nucleotides)

Allows bacteria to compete with each other.

#### Competency

Ability to do transformation.
**Naturally competent**:
	Can do transformation by themselves.
**Electro-competent**:
	Washed with salt, cooled.
	Electrical pulse opens temporary holes for plasmids to enter through.
	Plasmids are negatively changed, moving through an electrical gradient.
**Chemically competent**:
	Treated with $\ce{CaCl2}$/$\ce{MgCl2}$, helps plasmid to associate with the cell surface.
	Heat shock creates small holes.
	Plasmids get through the holes and the holes are sealed again in the cold.

#### In gram negative bacteria

DNA binds to the cell surface.
DNA pulled through.
1 strand gets inside the inner membrane, 1 is degraded.
ssDNA is protected.

## Transduction

Phages take some of the bacterial DNA with them while infecting them & reproducing.

**Generalised transduction** - random mis-packaging of any chromosomal DNA into phage head.
**Specialised transduction** - during bacteriophage lysogenic cycle a little DNA near the phage genes get packaged.

## Conjugation

Bacteria use [[Cell structure#Pilus|pili]] to transfer plasmids between each other.
Pili act like protein tubes between the bacteria.
When the plasmid goes through the pilus, it immediately gets replicated via the rolling circle method.

**F+ cells** - donors; have F-plasmids and pili.
**F- cells** - recipients; do not have F-plasmids nor pili.

**F-plasmid (fertility factor)** - encodes pilus and rolling circle replication genes.

> 2 F+ cells will not conjugate.

F-plasmid can be integrated into the chromosome.
Or the opposite: F-plasmid can take some chromosomal DNA to generate F'-plasmid.

## Genomic island

**Genomic island** - large parts of the genome that have been transferred by HGT.
Some are mobile, some lost mobility.
They have a different A=T/C=G ratio than the host genome - a way to tell they are acquired.

**Pathogenicity island** - genomic island used to infect other organisms.

## Barriers

**Mechanistic** - not all genes can be carried via these mechanisms + success is not guaranteed.

**Ecological** - bacteria must be in the same environment.

**Fitness** - no point in replicating DNA for someone else if it doesn't increase fitness.

**Compatibility** - both bacteria involved need to be able to use each other's DNA & proteins.
