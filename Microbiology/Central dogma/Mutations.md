
**Mutation** - change in DNA that **is not fixed**.
**Mutant** - organism with a mutation.

**Forward genetics** - phenotype → genotype
**Reverse genetics** - genotype → phenotype

Source of genetic variation, along with horizontal gene transfer.
Mutations occur randomly.

Mutations can be:
- **Beneficial** - fitness ↑
- **Neutral** - fitness --
- **Deleterious** - fitness ↓

## Types

**Point mutation** - change of 1 single nucleotide.
**Transition** - A ↔ G, C ↔ T; purine ↔ purine, pyrimidine ↔ pyrimidine
**Transversion** - A ↔ T, C ↔ G; pyrimidine ↔ purine

![[point-mutation.png]]

**Insertion** - 1 or more nucleotides are inserted.

**Deletion** - some amount of DNA is lost.

**Inversion** - the order of nucleotides is changed.

**Translocation** - rearrangement between non-homologous chromosomes.

## Causes

**AP site/abasic site** - a site without any of the nucleobases; apurinic/apyrimidinic.

**Mutagen** - cause of mutations.
**Carcinogen** - mutagen that causes cancer.

**Deamination** - loss of $\ce{-NH3}$ group on the nucleobase.
**Cause**: $\ce{HNO2}$
**Result**: AP site.

**Depurination** - loss of purine (A/G).
**Cause**: spontaneously
**Result**: apurinic site.

> Single stranded DNA cannot be repaired because there are no complementary nucleobases to reference.
> Important in cancer.

**Recombination** - DNA strands broken, but wrong ends are connected.

**Transposable elements** - move throughout the chromosome and influence nearby genes & regulation.

**Nucleoside analogs** - nucleosides that are structurally similar to the normal ones but pair incorrectly.
**Cause**: various chemicals
**Result**: during the next round of replication the pair will be changed.

**Intercalating agents** - insert themselves in between of the nucleobases.
**Cause**: exposure.
**Result**: DNA distorted, DNA polymerase may delete or insert new nucleobases (causes frame shift).

> **Acridine orange** is used in the lab to visualise DNA molecules.

**Ionising radiation** - creates hydroxyl radicals that affect DNA.
**Cause**: exposure to x-rays & gamma-rays.
**Result**: breaks in DNA backbone, deamination.

**Non-ionising radiation** - can be detrimental to DNA as well.
**Cause**: exposure to UV light.
**Result**: thymine dimers, T=T.

## Consequences

Mutations can affect promoters, amino acids, etc.

In the coding region:
- **Silent** - nothing changes, usually in the wobble position.
- **Missense** - AA → AA, protein efficacy changed depending on how close the AAs are in properties.
- **Nonsense** - AA → stop, protein often non-functional.
- **Insertion/Deletion** - shifts the entire reading frame, difficult to predict effect.
- **Extension** - stop codon deleted, difficult to predict effect.

**Conditional mutation** - effect only visible under specific conditions.

The overall transcribed mRNA stability & structure are also affected.

## Repair

**Exonuclease** - enzyme that removes nucleobases from DNA.

**DNA damage** - any chemical deviation from the standard state of DNA.
DNA damage only becomes a mutation when it's inherited.

Rate of mutations in bacteria: $10^{-10}$ /bp/generation

Proofreading - DNA polymerase checks the nucleobases it inserts and can fix mistakes on its own.

**Methyl-directed mismatch repair**
After replication, the old strand is methylated, the new one is not for several minutes.
The incorrect nucleobases are bound, cut out, removed by exonuclease, the gap is filled by DNA polymerase and ligase.

**Nucleotide excision repair (dark repair)**
Fixes thymine dimers.
DNA scanned for a bulge in the backbone left by the T=T dimers, it's cut out, filled by DNA polymerase and ligase.

**Photoreactivation (light repair)**
Also fixed T=T dimers, but usually in plants (because they have to be in sunlight anyway).
**Photolyase** - enzyme that binds to T=T dimers, gets energy from visible light, and breaks the dimer.

**Base excision repair (BER)**
Fixes deamination & depurination
Recognised by **AP endonuclease**, then similar to dark repair.

## Notation

This one is only applicable for amino acid-encoding codons.

Deletion:
	$\Delta \text{[AA][position]}$
	Example: $\Delta \text{F508}$ - phenylalanine-encoding codon at the position 508 is deleted.

Substitution:
	$\text{[normal AA][position][new AA]}$
	Example: $\text{E6V}$ - glutamate at position 6 is substituted by valine.
