
## Binary fission

Asexual reproduction

Stages:
1. **DNA replication**.
2. **Cell elongation** - the cell doubles in size.
3. **Formation of division septum** - right in the middle of the cell a film is created.
4. **Cytokinesis** - **FtsZ** protein forms a ring around the septum, brings the cell walls together, and assists in the formation of new peptidoglycan.

## Generation time

Every cell makes 2.
**Generation time** - the time it takes for the population to double.
Varies depending on the species of the cell (genetics) and conditions.

> **Intrinsic growth rate** is called this way because it is specific for a given species and environment.

Growth phases (assuming the culture is closed off):
1. **Lag phase**
	Newly inoculated cells are adapting to the new medium and repairing themselves.
	No division yet.
2. **Log phase**
	Cells start replicating exponentially.
	This is when cells are most vulnerable and when antibiotics are used.
3. **Stationary phase**
	Resources start running out and waste is accumulating.
	Dynamic equilibrium between cells dividing and dying.
	Metabolism is configured for survival.
	Sporulating cells begin forming endospores.
4. **Death phase**
	Environment is no longer favourable.
	Cells die rapidly.
	Endospores form.

![[growth-stages.png|489]]

**Persister cell** - cell that becomes dormant under stress; dormant state is something in between a living cell and an endospore.

## Measurement

Several techniques are used to monitor bacterial growth.

#### Direct cell count

A drop of the growth medium is taken under the microscope onto a grid plate.
\# of bacteria per grid cell are counted.
Assuming that bacteria are homogenously distributed across the growth medium, the total \# of bacteria is calculated.

**Problem**: cannot tell at a glance which bacteria are alive and which are dead.
**Solution**: differential staining with a dye that only enters a cell, if the cell wall is broken.

#### Plate count

On an agar plate, every single bacterium births a colony (colony forming unit).
Growth medium is diluted lots of times and a sample is taken and cultivated.
\# of resulting colonies is counted.
Total \# of bacteria is calculated.

Plating methods:
- **Pour plate method** - the plate is swirled around.
- **Spread plate method** - the sample is distributed across the plate with a rod.

#### Extinction dilution

Statistical approach.
Relies on the pH changes of the medium as a result of metabolism of cells.

#### Spectrophotometer

Very common & simple.
Measured the cloudiness of the sample to estimate the concentration of bacteria in the sample.

**Problem**: biofilms & pigments synthesised by the bacteria interfere.

## Requirements

In order to grow happily, cells need appropriate:
- Oxygen level
- pH
- Temperature
- Osmotic pressure (saltiness)
- Barometric pressure
- Other organisms

![[bacteria-oxygen-requirements.png|660]]

**Aerobic bacteria** have evolved to deal with reactive oxygen species using **superoxide dismutase**, **peroxidase**, and **catalase**.
**Anaerobic bacteria** lack all of these.
**Aerotolerant anaerobes** only have **superoxide dismutase**.

pH changes affect macromolecule structures (DNA, lipids, proteins).
![[bacteria-ph-requirements.png]]

![[bacteria-temperature-requirements.png]]

Growth medium types:
- **Complex medium** - has everything a cell might usually need.
- **Defined medium** - only has the components the scientist specifically wants.
- **Enriched medium** - has additional chemicals to accommodate the experiment or the specific requirements of bacteria.

## Stopping growth

**Fomites** - surfaces.

| Protocol      | Definition                                 | Application                    | Agents                       |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Disinfection  | Reduces/destroys microbes on fomites       | Cleaning lab equipment         | Bleach, phenols, ...         |
| Sanitization  | Reduces microbes to safe levels            | Dishwashing, bathroom cleaning | Detergents                   |
| Sterilization | Destroys **all** microbes, spores, viruses | Cleaning surgical equipment    | Pressurized steam, radiation |
| Antisepsis    | Reduces microbes on skin by application    | Cleaning a wound               | Alcohols, hydrogen peroxide  |
| Degerming     | Reduces microbes by scrubbing              | Washing hands                  | Soap                         |

> Only **sterilization** kills everything!
> **Autoclave** - pressurized steam cleaner, temperatures $> 100 \degree \text{C}$

Antibiotics are used to kill growing microbes/reduce their \#.
**Antibiotic resistance**!

**D-value** - time to divide the \# of microbes by 10.
**Thermal death time (TDT)** - time to kill **all** microbes as a given temperature.

To test antimicrobial agent - put on filter paper & let culture grow; the paper should stay clean.
To test product for contamination - put a sample into a medium & try cultivating.
