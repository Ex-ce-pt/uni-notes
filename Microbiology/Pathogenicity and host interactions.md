
**Microbiome** - all microbes associated with a higher organism.
**Resident microbiota** - live permanently.
**Transient microbiota** - live temporarily.

Microbes occupy niches - they don't only produce helpful substances but also squeeze the harmful bacteria out.
Microbiota affects non-infectious diseases.

**Primary pathogen** - always infects.
**Opportunistic pathogen** - only infects when given an opportunity to.

What affects human microbiota:
- Age
- Food
- Area of the body
- Person-to-person

> Blood is sterile.

## Diseases

**Sign** - objective, measurable characteristic of the patient (e.g. temperature).
**Symptom** - subjective experience.
**Syndrome** - signs + symptoms specific to a disease.

**Infectious disease** - caused directly by pathogen.
- **Communicable** - spread between humans.
- **Non-communicable** - not spread between humans.
- **Iatrogenic** - contracted as the result of a medical procedure.
- **Nosocomial** - acquired in a hospital setting.
- **Zoonosis** - transmitted from animals to humans.
**Noninfectious disease** - not caused by pathogens (genetics, the environment, or immune system dysfunction).

**Acute disease** - happens fast (e.g. influenza).
**Chronic disease** - long (legally, from 6 months onward?)
**Latent disease** - pathogen dormant for a long time (e.g. herpes).

# Molecular Koch's postulates

Determining if a molecule secreted by a microorganism is harmful.

1. Phenotype of microorganism associated only w/ pathogenic strains of microorganism.
2. Suspected gene deactivated → measurable loss of pathogenicity.
3. Activation again → pathogenicity restored.

## Quantifying

ID$_{50}$ - median infectious dose; \# pathogenic cells to infect 50% of the group.
LD$_{50}$ - median lethal dose; \# pathogenic cells to kill 50% of the group.

## Pathogenesis

Stages:
- **Exposure**
	Contact w/ pathogen.
	Usually through mucosal surfaces/breaks in skin.
- **Adhesion**
	Pathogen binds to the inside of the body.
	**Adhesins** - proteins/glycoproteins that bind to receptors of host cells.
	Various types.
	Biofilm formation starts.
- **Invasion**
	Pathogen spreads.
	Exoenzymes at play.
- **Infection**
	Successful growth.
	Local - small area near entry.
	Focal - slightly spread.
	Systemic - spread throughout the body.
- **Transmission**
	Looking for other hosts.
	Skin, respiratory, urogenital, gastrointestinal.

## Virulence factors

For adhesion various adhesins are used.
Fimbriae, pili, etc.

**Exoenzymes** - extracellular enzymes.
Hyaluronidase - dissolves hyaluronic acid in between of the (human) cells.
Collagenase - dissolves collagen.

**Endotoxin** - lipopolysaccharide on the outer membrane of Gram$^-$ bacteria; released when divides or dies.
Triggers fever, immune system overreaction, possibly shock.

**Exotoxin** - secreted protein interacts w/ host receptors; mainly Gram$^+$ but some Gram$^-$; low LD$_{50}$.

**Mycotoxins** - exotoxins of fungi, some carcirogenic.

#### Intracellular targeting toxins

Exotoxins that get inside the cells to damage them.

**A-B exotoxin** - A-B complex; B subunit (binding) binds to the cell and gets the A subunit (activity) inside the cell.
E.g. diphtheria, cholera, botulinum, tetanus

**Enterotoxin** - targets intestines; makes cells push out $\ce{Cl-}$ → pushes out $\ce{Na+}$ to compensate → water leaves due to osmosis → diarrhea.

**Neurotoxin** - targets neurons; blocks release of neurotransmitters like acetylcholine, glycine, GABA, etc.

#### Extracellular targeting toxins

Exotoxins that target things outside the host cells.

**Superantigen** - overstimulates immune system; high fevers, low blood pressure, multi-organ failure, shock.

#### Survival

**Capsules** - shielding from immune system.
**Proteases** - degrade antibodies to keep being virulent.
**Coagulase** - triggers blood coagulation.
**Antigenic variation** - surface proteins are constantly changed to avoid the immune system.

**Antigenic drift** - point mutations; 1 AA changed.
**Antigenic shift** - major change, genetic reassortment; genes jumbled → totally new protein.
