
**Organelle** - an organ of a cell that serves a specific purpose.

#### Cytoplasm

The liquid that contains everything in the cell.
Composed of water & dissolved chemicals.

#### Plasma membrane

Envelopes the cytoplasm.
1st outer layer of the cell.
Various channels are used to transport chemicals back and forth through the membrane.

## Prokaryotes

No membrane-bound organelles, but possess **inclusions** (bubbles) with a similar function.

**Cell envelope** - cell membrane + cell wall + cell capsule
**Cell capsule** - around cell wall; polysaccarides & proteins

#### Endospores

**Sporulation** - forming an endospore.
**Germination** - endospore becoming vegetative.
If the environment is unfavourable, the cell sporulates.
Endospores can survive thousands of years.

| Vegetative cell  | Endospore           |
| ---------------- | ------------------- |
| Sensitive        | Extremely resistant |
| Can do Gram test | Need special stains |
| Water inside     | Dehydrated          |
| Grows            | Doesn't grow        |
| Metabolises      | No metabolism       |

#### Cell wall

Around plasma membrane.
Gives the cell **shape**, along with cytoskeleton.
Protects from osmotic pressure.

**Peptidoglycan** - major component; made of N-acetylglucosamine (NAG) and N-acetylmuramic acid (NAM).
Only in bacteria.

NAM has a tetrapeptide chain attached.

Gram's test:
1. Stain cells purple - all cells are purple.
2. Wash w/ organic solvent - negatives are easily washed, positives are not washed and stay purple.
3. Stain the cells red - negatives are red, positives remain purple.

| Gram's test             | Positive                                        | Negative                                                |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Color after test**    | $\textcolor{purple}{\text{purple}}$             | $\textcolor{red}{\text{red}}$                           |
| **Outer membrane**      | ✗                                               | ✓                                                       |
| **Peptidoglycan**       | $30-100 \space \text{nm}$                       | $< 4 \space \text{nm}$                                  |
| **Tetrapeptide chains** | Linked by pentaglycine                          | Directly linked                                         |
| **Unique traits**       | Embedded teichoic acid<br>like poles through it | Periplasmic space between<br>inner membrane & cell wall |

> Mnemonics:
> Membrane is slippery, peptidoglycan is sticky.
> Red means negative.
> If no 2nd membrane, have to compensate w/ peptidoglycan.

#### Nucleoid

Circular, haploid (unpaired) chromosome without a membrane.
Mainly encodes vital genes.
**Nucleoid-associated proteins (NAPs)** - histone-like proteins; help packing the chromosome.

#### Plasmids

Small circular extrachromosomal DNA.
Often hundreds in a single cell.
Mainly encodes adaptation genes.

#### Ribosomes

Synthesise proteins from mRNA.

Small subunit: 30S
Large subunit: 50S
Full ribosome: 70S

#### Fimbria
pl. fimbriae

Short protein tentacles.
Hundreds on a single cell.
Used for adhesion to surfaces.
In pathogens, to infect.

#### Pilus
pl. pili

Longer protein tentacles.
For attachment & to exchange DNA between cells.

#### Flagellum
pl. flagella

Very long protein tails.
Spin like a propeller to move the cell forward.
Cells move forward a little bit, tumble in place to rotate, and move forward again.

## Eukaryotes

Have organelles.
Compartmentalise their processes very much.

**Endocytosis** - cell membrane envelops something outside of the cell and creates a massive vesicle; only in eukaryotes.
- **Phagocytosis** - immune cells digest whole bacteria in lysosomes.
- **Pinocytosis** - almost all cells can drink.
- **Receptor-mediated** - receptor triggers it; used by hormones & viruses.

#### Nucleus

Contains multiple linear chromosomes.
**Nucleolus** - synthesises rRNA and partially assembles it.
**Nuclear pores** - Transport in-and-out.
**Nuclear lamina** - determines shape.
**Histones** - proteins the chromosomes are wrapped around of.

#### Ribosome

Synthesises proteins.
Size: 80S
- In cytoplasm - water-soluble proteins.
- Attached to the **rough endoplasmic reticulum** - membrane proteins.

#### Endoplasmic reticulum

System of tubules and cisternae.
- Rough ER - attached ribosomes make membrane proteins.
- Smooth ER - synthesis of lipids, carbohydrate metabolism, break down toxic compounds.

#### Golgi apparatus

- Gets vesicles from ER
- Chemically modifies lipids (e.g. adds carbohydrates).
- Sends vesicles to the membrane or other organelles.

#### Lysosome & Peroxisome

Lysosome breaks down general waste.
Peroxisome breaks down toxic waste: uric acid, amino acids, fatty acids.

#### Cytoskeleton

Structural support filaments.
Also composed of microtubules that can be used for transport.

#### Mitochondria

Aerobic respiration.
Have their own DNA & 70S ribosomes.

#### Chloroplasts

Photosynthesis.
Similar to mitochondria.
