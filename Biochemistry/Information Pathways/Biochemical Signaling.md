#Information

A cell needs to be able to respond to the changes in the environment.
In multicellular organisms, it could be other cells of the organism.

**Signal transduction** - biochemical chain of events leading to physical response.

**Primary messenger** - messenger coming from outside of the cell; e.g. a hormone.
**Second messenger** - messenger inside the cell.

General pathway: **Signal** → **Reception** → **Amplification** → **Transduction** → **Response(s)**

Features:
- **High specificity**.
- **Sensitivity** - the threshold for activation is very low.
- **Amplification** - the \# of signaling molecules grows rapidly letting the entire cell know of the signal.
- **Desensitization** - if the signal persists for too long, the cell stops responding.
- **Integration** - the signals from multiple receptors are combined.
- **Localized response** - only a small region of a cell can process the signal, if needed.

## Hormones

Very diverse group of chemicals.
Small molecules or proteins that are produced in one tissue and carried through the blood to other tissues.
Coordinate the metabolic activities of several tissues.
Examples: hunger, blood pressure, etc.

**Top-down hormonal signaling** - hormone released by the central nervous system.
E.g. - eyes causing the release of adrenaline.
**Bottom-up hormonal signaling** - hormone release by another organ to the central nervous system.
E.g. - stomach signals hunger.

## GPCR
**G-protein coupled receptor**

Very important signaling molecules.
Embedded in the cell membrane.
~350 different GPCRs in humans.
Often mutated in cancer.

Components:
	**Membrane receptor** - binds the hormone.
	**G-protein** (inside the cell) - releases second messengers.

G-proteins have $\ce{GDP}$, which they release when the receptor gets a signal and instead bind $\ce{GTP}$.
Then **Adenylate cyclase** is activated - it creates $\ce{\text{c}AMP}$ and phosphorylates enzymes to regulate them.

Different cells have different GPCRs, so every type of cell can be fine-tuned.

G-proteins are **GTPases** - they degrade $\ce{GTP}$ very slowly (seconds to minutes).

## Other pathways

Signaling can also happen by cleaving one of the groups of a triacylglycerol in the cell membrane.
PIP2 → DAG, IP3 - second messengers.

**Calmodulin** - protein that binds $\ce{Ca^{2+}}$ (acts as a second messenger).

**Tyrosine kinases** bind messengers & phosphorylate proteins all in one receptor.
E.g. insulin receptor.

Epidermal growth factor (EGF)
Long chain of phosphorylations to grow skin cells.

**Steroids** can diffuse through the membrane by themselves directly into the nucleus and bind to receptors inside.

## Desensitization

If the receptor is activated for a long time, the cell can swallow them.
This means that these receptors cannot be activated any more.
