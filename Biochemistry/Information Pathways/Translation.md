mRNA -> Protein

**Codon** - 3 adjacent nucleotides that code for a single amino acid.
64 codons possible.
3 **Stop** codons: **UAA**, **UAG**, **UGA**.
**AUG** codon is *usually* the initiation codon - encodes **Met** in eukaryotes and **fMet** in prokaryotes.

> **fMet** is N-formylmethionine.

No delimiters; nucleotides are continuously read 3 at a time (non-overlapping frame).

**Degenerate genetic code** - some amino acids are specified by more than 1 codon.

mRNA has a **leader segment** and a **trailer segment** - the ends of the mRNA lie beyond the initiation and stop codons.

### Codon positions

2nd position:
*Generally*, codes for type of amino acid.
- U - hydrophobic
- C - small & polar
- A - hydrophilic

**Wobble position** - the 3rd position in the codon; usually not important.
Mutations usually happen in the wobble position because it doesn't change the function of the protein.
This position is also further from the anticodon on the tRNA, so it doesn't bind perfectly.

**Codon bias** - if multiple codons are available for an amino acid, organisms prefer one codon more than others.
In this case there's more tRNA that corresponds to the preferred codon.

## Exceptions in codon interpretation

In human mitochondria:
- **UGA** → **Trp** instead of **Stop**
- **AGA** & **AGG** → **Stop** instead of **Arg**
- **AUA** → **Met** instead of **Ile**

Codons can be interpreted in different ways depending on context.

When synthesising Glutathione peroxidase:
**UGA** → **selenocysteine** instead of **Stop**

## tRNA

Carries amino acid + anticodon

**Anticodon** - a sequence of 3 nucleotides complementary to the codon the amino acid of the tRNA is encoded with.
**Charging** - adding the amino acid to the tRNA.

Nomenclature:
	Empty: $\text{tRNA}^{\text{AA}}$, e.g. $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Ala}}$
	Charged: $\text{AA-tRNA}^{\text{AA}}$, e.g. $\text{Ala-tRNA}^{\text{Ala}}$

**Aminoacylation** - technical term for charging the tRNA.
**Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase** - enzyme that performs aminoacylation.
One of them for every amino acid.
	**Class I** - attaches AA to 2'-OH group of tRNA.
	**Class II** - attaches AA to 3'-OH group of tRNA.

$\ce{AA + ATP -> AA-AMP -> AA-\text{tRNA}}$

**Isoaccepting tRNA** - tRNA that accepts the same amino acid, but has a different anticodon (needed because of the degenerate DNA).

Sometimes there are errors, but they are not significant.
Error-correction mechanisms also exist.

If there are no tRNA to carry a specific amino acid, the amino acid can be synthesised on the tRNA itself:
- Glu → Gln
- Ser → SeCys
- Met → fMet

**Inosine (I)** - modified nucleotide specifically for tRNA; can pair with A, U, C.

## Ribosome

Made of rRNA and proteins.
Eukaryotes have larger ones.
2 subunits: small & large.

Assembled in nucleolus.
**Nucleolus** - non-membrane-bound organelle in the cell nucleus.

Sites:
- A site - aminoacyl site; amino acid to be put into the protein.
- P site - peptidyl site; amino acid that was just added to protein.
- E site - exit site; tRNA without amino acid.

The initiation starts with mRNA being fit into the small subunit with the help of initiation factors.
Then the large subunit attaches and the initiation factors are released.
**Ribosome binding site** - small sequence on the mRNA that binds to the ribosome to start translation.
**Shine-Dalgarno sequence** - ribosome binding site for prokaryotes.
**Kozak consensus sequence** - ribosome binding site for eukaryotes.

Elongation factors recognise the correct tRNA and bind them to the A site.

Release factors help recognise the **Stop** codon & release the protein and mRNA.

> mRNA sometimes goes from one ribosome directly into another ribosome, synthesising the same protein multiple times in parallel.

> In bacteria, transcription can also be happening at the same time as translation.

## Post-translational processing

#### Proteolytic cleavage

Cutting proteins.
Secreted proteins are activated by cleaving off the part that deactivates them.
E.g. **trypsin/chymotrypsin** - digestive enzymes.
Common in viruses.
Can also chop up longer peptides into multiple standalone proteins.

#### Chemical modification

Changes the charge or polarity of the protein.

- Methylation
- Acetylation
- Glycosylation
- Phosphorylation
- Ubiquitination

#### Targeting

Sorting sequences of amino acids in the protein make them bind to other molecules that take them where they are needed.

Secreted proteins are synthesised in the rough endoplasmic reticulum and transferred out.

#### Degradation

**Ubiquitin** is the signal for degradation.
Proteins are then degraded by **proteasomes**.
