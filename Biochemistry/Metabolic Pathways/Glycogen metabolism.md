#Metabolism 

## Glycogen metabolism

#### Glycogenolysis

Most energy is stored in fat, but it cannot be used to power the brain.
Glycogen is used for quick energy in brain and muscles.
Glycogen is stored in liver and muscle cells.

> When starving, glycogen is depleted.

[[Molecules of Life/Carbohydrates#Glycogen|Glycogen]]
50 000 glucose subunits, 2 000 ends for enzymes to act upon.
Protein dimer in the middle - **glycogenin**.

**Glycogen phosphorylase** cleaves off a glucose.
**Glucose-1P** is generated.
**Phosphoralysis reaction** - the glucose is cleaved by adding a $\ce{Pi}$ group in the place of the glycosidic bond.

Glycogen phosphorylase keeps going until 4 glucoses away from a branching point.
The branch doesn't fit into the enzyme any more.

**Glycogen debranching enzyme** - transfers 3 glucoses to the straight chain, cleaves off the last glucose off the branch.

**Phosphoglucomutase** - transfers $\ce{Pi}$ group from $\ce{C}1$ to $\ce{C}6$; $\ce{glucose-1P -> glucose-6P}$

**Glucose-6P** enters [[Metabolic Pathways/Glycolysis|glycolysis]].

Liver exports glucose into the bloodstream.
**Glucose-6-phosphatase** - enzyme in the liver; $\ce{glucose-6P -> glucose + Pi}$
It is located in the endoplasmic reticulum of the liver cell as to not counteract glycolysis.

#### Synthesis

**UDP-glucose** - the activated glucose form used for glycogen synthesis.
UDP is a good leaving group

$\ce{UTP + glucose-1P → UDP-glucose + PPi}$

The synthesis process starts with glycogenin.
It binds a couple UDP-glucose molecules to its **Tyr** sidechains.
Then it adds a couple more glucose molecules form UDP-glucose.
Then other enzymes take over polymerization.

**Glycogen synthase** - extends the glycogen chains with $\alpha$-1,4 links.
**Branching enzyme** - branches the glycogen chains with $\alpha$-1,6 links.

#### Regulation

**Glycogen phosphorylase** is regulated by phosphorylation - literally removing the $\ce{Pi}$ group it would be donating to the glucose.
Glucose itself binds to allosteric sites of glycogen phosphorylase to promote dephosphorylation.

Glucagon & epinephrine → breaking down.
Glucose & insulin → synthesis.
