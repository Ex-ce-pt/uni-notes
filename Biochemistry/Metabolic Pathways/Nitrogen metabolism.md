#Metabolism 


$\ce{N2}$, although abundant, is very inert.
$\ce{NH3}$ is the fixed nitrogen and a source for biosynthesis.

## Fixation

**Diazotrophs** - microorganisms that fix atmospheric nitrogen.

**Nitrogenase complex** - catalyses stepwise $e^-$ transfer onto nitrogen for fixation.

**Glutamine synthetase** - $\ce{Glu + ATP + NH3 -> Gln + ADP + Pi}$
Highly regulated enzyme by various compounds.
From there nitrogen is transferred to other molecules.

> In animals glutamate comes from AA catabolism.

Carbon skeletons come from catabolism/anabolism pathways.

#### Mammals

Dietary protein can be oxidised for energy. Mainly by carnivores.
For humans, mainly if starving or excess of AA ingested.

**Pepsin** - nonspecific protease, active at pH = 2, degrades proteins into AAs.
In stomach

**Trypsin**, **Chymotrypsin** are other proteases.
Secreted by the pancreas, only get activated in the intestines to protein from self-digestion.

The transporters on cells (specific) take AAs inside.

AA catabolism largely takes place in the liver.

## Urea cycle

Ammonia is toxic and needs to be removed.

Excretion:
- Fish - secrete $\ce{NH3}$ into the water by dilution.
- Ureotelic animals - produce urea via the urea cycle.
- Birds/reptiles - produce uric acid.

1. **Aminotransferases** - move $\ce{-NH2}$ from AAs onto $\alpha$-ketoglutarate, producing **Glu**.
2. **Glutamate dehydrogenase** - takes the $\ce{-NH2}$ from Glu and forms $\ce{NH3}$.
3. Urea Cycle
	One $\ce{-NH2}$ comes from carbomoyl-P, one from **Asp**
	Carbamoyl synthetase - rate limiting!

Urea cycle in the liver, but only 2 steps happen in the mitochondria, everything else in the cytosol.

The carbon skeletons of the AA are metabolised in the [[Metabolic Pathways/Citric Acid Cycle (TCA Cycle)|TCA cycle]].

Aminotransferares are specific, cofactor - **PLP**
